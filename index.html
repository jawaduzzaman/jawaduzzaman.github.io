Origin Story

A People Leader Who Started as a Builder

Most HR careers run in a straight line. Mine didn't — and that's the point.

I started as a self-taught graphic designer in 2013, then spent years in IT support and accounting — learning how systems and numbers actually work before I ever wrote a job description. A stint in data research (IBM SPSS, predictive analytics, COVID-19 studies) and digital marketing (SEO, paid campaigns, funnel analytics) followed. By the time I landed in HR in 2021, I wasn't thinking like a generalist — I was thinking like someone who builds tools.

That's exactly what I've done at NEXT Ventures: three internal promotions in 3.5 years, from Executive HRBP to Manager I overseeing People & Culture — not by managing harder, but by engineering smarter. When I joined, there was no ATS, no HRIS, no performance infrastructure of any kind. I identified those gaps, built the business case with hard data — projecting exactly how much efficiency each system would recover and at what cost — then proposed, secured approval for, implemented, and owned all three. The ATS went from concept to full operational efficiency. The HRIS was built entirely from scratch, every configuration mine, and driven to complete organisational adoption. The performance system became the live intelligence layer leadership now relies on daily instead of waiting for quarterly retrospectives.

The result is a profile most organisations rarely get to hire for: someone who commands both the human side of HR strategy and the architecture of the systems that power it — from designing workflow logic inside ATS platforms, to engineering HRIS data pipelines and ETL processes, to deploying predictive workforce analytics using statistical modelling. Not a hybrid of two skill sets loosely stapled together. A single professional who moves fluidly between boardroom talent strategy and the back-end configuration of the platforms that execute it at scale.

Straight Answers

Questions Recruiters Ask Me

Why does an HR manager write code?

Because every major HR problem I've encountered was secretly a systems problem — data trapped in spreadsheets, decisions made on memory, hours lost to manual work. Code and automation multiply what one HR team can deliver. I'd rather build the machine than be the machine.

Can you rebuild these systems at a new company?

Yes — that's the point of the four-phase loop. Diagnose, business case, build, automate: the method is platform-agnostic. Recruitee, HiBob and Revolut were the right tools for one context; the process that shipped them transfers to any stack, any org.

What kind of environment do you thrive in?

Fast-moving organisations where the infrastructure doesn't exist yet. I built my track record in FX/fintech pace — compliance-heavy, scaling quickly, zero tolerance for guesswork. If your people systems are a blank page, that's my favourite starting condition.

Remote, hybrid, or on-site?

Open globally. APAC-based, remote-first ready, and comfortable operating across time zones — my systems are built to run asynchronously, and so am I.
